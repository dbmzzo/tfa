Rules Dispute

Should half-credit count when a finals answer has the right person but wrong title?

Started by PubJudgeMara | 14 replies

Collapse
PubJudgeMara League scorekeeper

Last night a team answered "Harriet Tubman, Secretary of the Underground Railroad." Obviously the office is wrong, but the person is clearly right. Our house rule says finals answers must be "substantially complete." I gave half-credit and now two teams are appealing.

StackedChips Tuesday captain

If the category was historical titles, I would mark it wrong. If the category was famous abolitionists, I would allow the identification and ignore the invented title. Context matters more than the stray wording.

TriviaDent Question editor

TFA Appeal Note 4.2 usually asks whether the wrong detail creates a new false claim that changes the answer's meaning. In this case it does, so I would have gone with zero unless your league handbook explicitly allows split scoring.

Question Quality

Ambiguous moon question in Round 3: does "largest moon" need a planet named?

Started by CosmicPam | 9 replies

Collapse
CosmicPam Regional finalist

The prompt just asked for "the largest moon in the solar system." My team wrote Ganymede. Another team wrote Titan and argued the host never specified "current accepted measurements" versus "largest visible apparent size." Nobody at the table had ever heard that distinction before.

BellringerLou Host

That is a badly phrased question, full stop. If a host leaves room for alternate interpretations, the safest ruling is to void it and make the round score out of one fewer point.

NorthTableNina Stats volunteer

Our circuit added a proofreading checklist after this exact type of problem. Any superlative question now gets a required qualifier line: by area, by population, by distance, by mass, and so on.

Buzzer latency at the Lakeside Invitational changed three tossups

Started by RedPenWalter | 11 replies

Expand
RedPenWalter Moderator

We reviewed the recording and there is a consistent delay between the room audio and the scoring console. It is small, but in a speed format that is enough to reverse at least three buzzes. Has anyone seen TFA issue a formal console calibration standard?

FretboardIris Tech marshal

Not a universal standard, but several invitationals now do a pre-match latency check with a dummy light and synchronized video. If the hardware is older than the house mixer, you are asking for trouble.

QuizMistress77 Former appeals chair

I would replay only the affected tossups if both teams can be reseated quickly. Replaying the entire match sounds cleaner on paper, but it punishes everyone for a wiring error.

Standings

Do retroactive penalties belong in season standings after a leaked packet?

Started by BracketMoss | 7 replies

Expand
BracketMoss League organizer

We found out two weeks later that one semifinal packet had been shared in a private group chat. There is no proof the first-place team opened it, but the distribution itself is documented. Do you freeze the standings or strip results anyway to protect the league?

ArchivistLen Rules historian

Transparency beats certainty here. Publish the timeline, void the packet if contamination is plausible, and explain why the remedy is procedural rather than accusatory. Players can live with a replay more easily than a secretive ruling.

SidePotSal Bar league captain

Retroactive penalties should be the last resort. If you cannot identify an advantaged team, punish the process that failed, not the standings table.

Historical Rumors

Old rumor: was the TFA founded out of the same salons as the Order of St. Apollonia?

Started by CandleQuizzer | 22 replies

Expand
CandleQuizzer Archive digger

I keep seeing references in old newsletter scans to "the Apollonian rooms" where early fairness committees met. Was that just a rented salon, or is this where the stories about the TFA being tied to secret societies started?

ArchivistLen Rules historian

Most evidence points to literary clubs borrowing symbolic language, not an actual clandestine order running trivia governance. Early associations loved ceremonial names, wax seals, and overly dramatic minutes.

VelvetGavel Convention regular

The rumor persists because the founders apparently held midnight rules readings during the 1928 congress. That sounds mysterious until you realize they were just trying to finish before the train schedule changed.

NorthTableNina Stats volunteer

I like the version where the "inner circle" was simply the proofreading committee with a private key to the filing cabinet. Secret society is more fun, but bad records management is usually the real answer.

Conspiracy Corner

Why does every third TFA crest have owls, keys, or stars on it?

Started by SignalFlareJo | 18 replies

Expand
SignalFlareJo Late-night poster

Owls for wisdom, keys for access, stars for rank. I know, I know, it could all be normal symbolism, but you cannot tell me the 1963 crest does not look like it belongs on a velvet robe in a basement lodge.

PubJudgeMara League scorekeeper

Counterpoint: every civic club between 1900 and 1970 used the exact same symbols. If the TFA is a secret society, then so was my grandfather's bowling association.

BellringerLou Host

The best explanation I have heard is that an early emblem designer recycled iconography from academic medals and fraternal print catalogs. That is much less thrilling than a hidden council, but probably much closer to the truth.
